Frequency Analysis

Learn to analyze ciphertext and break substitution ciphers using letter frequency distributions.

Aim & Objectives

Aim: To understand and apply the principles of frequency analysis to analyze and attempt to decrypt a monoalphabetic substitution cipher.


Objectives:

  • Understand what frequency analysis is and why it is useful in cryptography.
  • Learn how letter frequencies can help identify possible plaintext substitutions.
  • Analyze a given ciphertext to generate a letter-frequency table and graph.
  • Map ciphertext letters to probable plaintext letters by comparing with standard English frequencies.

Theory

Monoalphabetic Substitution Cipher: A cipher where each letter of the plaintext is replaced with a single corresponding letter of the ciphertext. For example, all 'A's might become 'Q's.


What is Frequency Analysis?

In any given language, certain letters appear more frequently than others. In English, the letter 'E' is the most common, followed by 'T', 'A', 'O', 'I', 'N', 'S', 'H', and 'R'. Because a monoalphabetic substitution cipher does not change the frequency of letters (it merely re-labels them), we can count the occurrences of each letter in the ciphertext and compare those counts to standard English letter frequencies.


Frequency Percentage:

Frequency Percentage = (character count / total alphabetic characters) × 100

Why Frequency Analysis Works:

By finding the most common letter in the ciphertext, we can guess that it might represent 'E' or 'T'. The next most common might represent the other. By mapping a few common letters, we can start to see partial words, allowing us to guess the rest using contextual and pattern analysis.


Limitations:

Frequency analysis works best on long ciphertexts. In short messages, the letter frequencies may not match average statistical distributions. For instance, a short message might not contain a single 'E'. Therefore, contextual analysis (looking at word lengths, repeated letters, common prefixes/suffixes) is critical to refine the mapping.

Procedure

  1. Enter a custom ciphertext or select one of the predefined samples in the Simulation tab.
  2. Click the Analyze Frequency button to process the ciphertext.
  3. Review the generated letter-frequency table and the bar chart.
  4. Compare your results with the standard English letter frequencies provided in the reference table.
  5. Identify probable substitutions (e.g., mapping the most frequent ciphertext letter to 'E' or 'T').
  6. Enter your guessed plaintext letters into the substitution mapping workspace.
  7. Click Apply Mapping to see the partially decrypted plaintext.
  8. If you selected a predefined sample, you can click Check Answer to verify your mappings.
  9. Use the Hint button if you get stuck.
  10. Click Reset to start over.
  11. Finally, attempt the assessment quiz to test your knowledge.
